# 📦 Chapter 3: Inventory & Trading Transactions

> [!NOTE]
> **Scope & Pedagogy**: This chapter covers the double-entry accounting rules for goods purchased specifically for resale (Inventory/Stock). It introduces the four core trading accounts: Purchases, Sales, Returns Outwards, and Returns Inwards. It excludes expense/revenue accounts and final accounting adjustments, covered in Chapters 4, 7, and 9.

---

## 3.1 Definition of Inventory (Stock)

In financial accounting, **Inventory** consists strictly of merchandise acquired for the explicit purpose of **resale at a profit**.

- A motor vehicle bought by a car dealer for showroom resale is **Inventory** (Purchases).
- A motor vehicle bought by a business for delivery operations is a **Non-Current Asset** (Motor Van Account).

---

## 3.2 The Four Core Inventory Accounts

Instead of recording inventory movements directly into a single asset account during the period, four specialized accounts are maintained:

1. **Purchases Account**: Records all acquisitions of goods meant for resale (Cash purchases & Credit purchases).
2. **Sales Account**: Records all disposals of goods held for resale (Cash sales & Credit sales).
3. **Returns Outwards (Purchases Returns) Account**: Records goods returned by the business back to credit suppliers.
4. **Returns Inwards (Sales Returns) Account**: Records goods returned to the business by credit customers.

---

## 3.3 Double-Entry Rules for Trading Transactions

$$\begin{array}{|l|l|l|}
\hline
\textbf{Transaction Type} & \textbf{Debit Account (Dr)} & \textbf{Credit Account (Cr)} \\ \hline
\textbf{Cash Purchase} & \text{Purchases Account} & \text{Cash / Bank Account} \\ \hline
\textbf{Credit Purchase} & \text{Purchases Account} & \text{Supplier Account (Trade Payable)} \\ \hline
\textbf{Cash Sale} & \text{Cash / Bank Account} & \text{Sales Account} \\ \hline
\textbf{Credit Sale} & \text{Customer Account (Trade Receivable)} & \text{Sales Account} \\ \hline
\textbf{Returns Outwards} & \text{Supplier Account (Trade Payable)} & \text{Returns Outwards Account} \\ \hline
\textbf{Returns Inwards} & \text{Returns Inwards Account} & \text{Customer Account (Trade Receivable)} \\ \hline
\end{array}$$

## 3.4 Comprehensive Worked Example: Ledger Records for Inventory

### Transactions for July:
- July 1: Started business with $\$3,800$ cash.
- July 3: Bought goods for cash $\$480$.
- July 7: Bought goods on credit from J. Gill for $\$1,200$.
- July 10: Sold goods for cash $\$172$.
- July 14: Returned goods to J. Gill $\$240$.
- July 18: Bought goods on credit from F. Genesis for $\$1,460$.
- July 21: Returned goods to F. Genesis $\$104$.
- July 24: Sold goods on credit to A. Prince for $\$292$.
- July 25: Paid J. Gill $\$960$ cash in full settlement of account balance.
- July 31: A. Prince paid us $\$292$ in cash.

## 3.5 Key Summary & Distinction

> [!KEY-CONCEPT]
> **Summary Rules**:
> - **Purchases** $\implies$ Always **Debit** balance (increases stock available for sale).
> - **Sales** $\implies$ Always **Credit** balance (revenue generated from stock).
> - **Returns Outwards** $\implies$ Always **Credit** balance (reduces effective purchases).
> - **Returns Inwards** $\implies$ Always **Debit** balance (reduces effective sales).
