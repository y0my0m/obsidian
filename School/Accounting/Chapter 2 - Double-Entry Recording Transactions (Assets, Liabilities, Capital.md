# 🧾 Chapter 2: Double-Entry Recording Transactions (Assets, Liabilities, Capital

> [!NOTE]
> **Scope & Pedagogy**: This chapter introduces the mechanics of double-entry bookkeeping using ledger accounts (T-accounts). It focuses on recording transactions for Assets, Liabilities, and Capital. It builds on Chapter 1 without incorporating revenue/expense accounts or inventory adjustments, which are detailed in Chapters 3 and 4.

---

## 2.1 The Nature of a Transaction: Item Exchanged & Form of Settlement

Every business transaction consists of two fundamental elements:
1. **The Item Exchanged**: The physical resource, service, or asset acquired or disposed of.
2. **The Form of Settlement**: How the transaction is settled (Cash, Bank cheque, Credit/IOU, or Capital contribution).

---

## 2.2 Luca Pacioli's Double-Entry System & Rules of Debit and Credit

In 1494, Luca Pacioli codified the double-entry bookkeeping framework. A ledger account is formatted as a **T-Account**:

**Account Name**

| Debit (left) | Credit (right) |
| --- | --- |
| **Debit (Dr)** | **Credit (Cr)** |
| (Left Hand Side) | (Right Hand Side) |

### Core Rules for Assets, Liabilities, and Capital

| **Account Type** | **To Record an INCREASE (+)** | **To Record a DECREASE (-)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Asset Account** | DEBIT (Dr) | CREDIT (Cr) |
| **Liability Account** | CREDIT (Cr) | DEBIT (Dr) |
| **Capital Account** | CREDIT (Cr) | DEBIT (Dr) |

### Form of Settlement Focus Rule
When recording any transaction:
- If the **Form of Settlement** is **given / paid out** $\implies$ **Credit** the settlement account (Cash/Bank).
- If the **Form of Settlement** is **received / paid in** $\implies$ **Debit** the settlement account (Cash/Bank).
- The **Item Exchanged** is recorded on the **opposite side** in its respective account.

## 2.3 Recording Asset, Liability, and Capital Transactions

### Example 1: Starting Business with Capital
*Transaction*: June 1: P. Bernard started business with $\$12,000$ cash.
- Form of Settlement: Cash received $\implies$ Debit Cash Account $$\$12,000$$.
- Item Exchanged: Capital contributed $\implies$ Credit Capital Account $$\$12,000$$.

### Example 2: Paying Cash into Bank
*Transaction*: June 2: Paid $\$11,700$ cash into a new business bank account.
- Cash given $\implies$ Credit Cash Account $$\$11,700$$.
- Bank received $\implies$ Debit Bank Account $$\$11,700$$.

### Example 3: Purchasing an Asset on Credit
*Transaction*: June 5: Purchased office furniture on credit from Dream Ltd for $\$1,900$.
- Asset acquired $\implies$ Debit Office Furniture Account $$\$1,900$$.
- Credit obligation incurred $\implies$ Credit Dream Ltd (Trade Payable) Account $$\$1,900$$.

## 2.5 Exam Summary & Key Takeaways

> [!EXAM-TRAP]
> **Debit vs. Credit Summary**:
> - **Debit (Left)**: Increase in Assets, Decrease in Liabilities, Decrease in Capital.
> - **Credit (Right)**: Decrease in Assets, Increase in Liabilities, Increase in Capital.
> - **Always check**: Every entry must have an equal and opposite entry $$\sum \text{Debits} = \sum \text{Credits}$$.
